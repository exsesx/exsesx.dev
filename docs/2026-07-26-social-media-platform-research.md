# LinkedIn and X guidance for promoting a technical blog

Date checked: 2026-07-26

Scope: current first-party guidance from LinkedIn and X. This note separates
platform-documented behavior from suggested experiments. It does not assess the
blog's existing articles or social accounts.

## Executive recommendation

Run a four-week organic test built around one useful idea per post:

- On LinkedIn, publish two substantive posts per week. LinkedIn says members who
  post twice weekly see up to five times more profile views, and recommends
  authentic, actionable knowledge drawn from personal expertise and experience
  ([LinkedIn: Build your voice](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7456132)).
- On X, start with three distinct posts per article across 7–10 days: a concise
  launch post, a standalone technical lesson or short thread, and a
  question/result post. Add one later evergreen reshare only if the topic remains
  useful. This is a test cadence, not an X rule: X asks accounts to post
  consistently and frequently, plan with a content calendar, schedule coverage,
  and let analytics determine what resonates
  ([X: Get your business started](https://business.x.com/en/basics/get-your-business-started-with-x),
  [X: Organic best practices](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices)).
- Optimize for qualified visits and conversations, not raw impressions. Add
  campaign-specific UTM parameters to article URLs and compare social-platform
  data with completed visits in site analytics. X explicitly warns that its link
  clicks can exceed visits recorded by a website because X counts the click
  before the destination finishes loading
  ([X: Analytics discrepancies](https://business.x.com/en/help/campaign-measurement-and-analytics/common-analytics-discrepancies)).

## LinkedIn

### Content and formats

LinkedIn's first-party creator guidance favors posts that:

- share unique knowledge, expertise, opinions, experiences, or anecdotes;
- help other professionals learn or do their jobs better;
- are authentic and engaging; and
- provide an actionable insight or takeaway
  ([LinkedIn: Build your voice](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7456132)).

LinkedIn additionally recommends posting regularly, remaining
non-promotional, varying formats, and continuing the conversation through
meaningful replies to comments
([LinkedIn: Top Voices best practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a776208)).
That supports a practical technical-blog format: lead with the problem or
surprising result, teach the core lesson natively, give one concrete example,
then ask a genuine question or invite the reader to the full article.

A standard LinkedIn post allows up to 3,000 characters. Available formats
include text, photos, video, documents/PDF slideshows, polls, articles, and
newsletters
([LinkedIn: Post and share updates](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528176),
[LinkedIn: Build your voice](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7456132)).
Use a PDF/document post when a diagram, checklist, or step sequence is useful
without leaving the feed; use a link post when article traffic is the primary
goal.

### Links and previews

LinkedIn creates a preview image from a shared URL. Its current photo-sharing
documentation says a post can use either a URL preview or an uploaded image,
not both; a photo post can contain up to 20 images
([LinkedIn: Share photos](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a527229/sharing-photos-or-videos?lang=en)).
For Page posts, a super or content admin can customize a URL preview's title
and image; LinkedIn recommends a 1.91:1 image at 1200×627 pixels, more than 200
pixels wide
([LinkedIn: Customize a Page post preview](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a566445)).

There is no support in the current first-party guidance reviewed here for the
common claim that putting the article URL in the first comment improves organic
reach. LinkedIn's documented workflow is to share the article URL in the post
and let the platform create its preview
([LinkedIn: Share Content help index](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/topic/a148004)).
Treat first-comment placement as an unproven experiment, not a default.

If LinkedIn shows an outdated preview image, its Post Inspector can refresh the
cached URL preview
([LinkedIn: Share Content help index](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/topic/a148004)).

### Native media

For an uploaded photo, LinkedIn permits up to 5 MB, requires at least 552×276
pixels, recommends 1,080 pixels wide, and accepts aspect ratios from 3:1 to 4:5.
Uploaded images cannot be edited or resized after posting
([LinkedIn: Share photos](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a527229/sharing-photos-or-videos?lang=en)).
Add alt text in the composer; the same Help page exposes that option for
screen-reader users.

For document posts, supported formats include PDF, PPT/PPTX, DOC/DOCX, and
others, with a 100 MB and 300-page ceiling
([LinkedIn: Supported media types](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a564109/media-file-types-supported-on-linkedin?lang=en)).
For promotion, a short, focused PDF derived from one article is more usable
than uploading the whole article.

### Cadence, scheduling, and conversation

LinkedIn's clearest member-level cadence anchor is twice per week: members who
post that often see up to five times more profile views on average
([LinkedIn: Build your voice](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7456132)).
This is an association reported by LinkedIn, not a guarantee of traffic.

LinkedIn's native scheduler can publish a standard post between one hour and
three months in advance. Personal scheduled posts do not support events, jobs,
or service posts
([LinkedIn: Scheduled posting](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1364501)).
Schedule for consistency, but publish when there is time to answer comments:
LinkedIn explicitly recommends continuing conversations through meaningful
comment engagement
([LinkedIn: Top Voices best practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a776208)).

### Analytics

All members have combined post analytics for text, image, video, event, poll,
and article content. The view supports date ranges from 7 to 365 days, charts
impressions or engagements, ranks top posts, and exports `.XLSX`
([LinkedIn: Combined post analytics](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a701208)).
Individual post analytics includes impressions, distinct members reached,
in-network versus out-of-network distribution, social engagement, and—when the
privacy threshold is met—viewer demographics such as role, location, industry,
company size, and seniority. LinkedIn notes that these figures are estimates
([LinkedIn: Post analytics](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a516971/post-analytics-for-your-content)).

For a blog-growth test, review:

1. out-of-network reach;
2. comments, saves, sends, and reposts;
3. profile views/follower growth; and
4. UTM-tagged article sessions and engaged reading on the site.

The first three diagnose LinkedIn distribution; the fourth measures the actual
blog outcome.

## X

### Content and formats

X officially recommends concise, conversational copy, a clear call to action
such as "Read the full story," and native media to help a post stand out
([X: Organic best practices](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices)).
A standard post supports 280 characters and up to four total media items
([X: How to post](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/how-to-post)).

For a technical article, use:

- a single-post version for one sharp claim, lesson, or result;
- a two- or three-post thread for a compact explanation; or
- a native image/video for a demo, before/after, diagram, or measured result.

X says threads of two or three posts generally appear together in the timeline,
while threads of four or more are truncated behind "Show this thread." Put the
hook and the most useful point in the first two posts
([X: Create a thread](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/create-a-thread)).

X's current first-party hashtag guidance is inconsistent. Its organic best
practices say to avoid hashtags, while its getting-started guide says to limit
them to one or two if used
([X: Organic best practices](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices),
[X: Get your business started](https://business.x.com/en/basics/get-your-business-started-with-x)).
Use no hashtag by default; test one precise topic hashtag only where it helps a
real conversation.

### Links and previews

Every URL posted to X is routed through `t.co` and consumes 23 characters,
regardless of its original length; this cannot be disabled
([X: How to post a link](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/how-to-post-a-link)).
Keep the article URL in the launch post with a specific promise about what the
reader will learn. Current official organic guidance does not endorse moving
the link to the first reply as a reach tactic; it recommends a clear CTA in the
post itself
([X: Organic best practices](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices)).

The current basic posting-link documentation does not guarantee how an
arbitrary external page's preview card will render. Test each article URL in
the composer before scheduling it
([X: How to post a link](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/how-to-post-a-link)).
X also documents Website Buttons/Cards, created through Ads Manager, which can
make an image or video clickable and can be used organically
([X Ads glossary](https://business.x.com/en/help/overview/x-ads-glossary)).
They are optional; do not make Ads Manager setup a prerequisite for the first
organic test.

### Native media

X permits up to four media items. Photos can be up to 5 MB; GIFs can be up to 5
MB on mobile and 15 MB on web. A single photo with an aspect ratio from 2:1 to
3:4 displays in full
([X: Photos and GIFs](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/posting-gifs-and-pictures),
[X: How to post](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/how-to-post)).

Add alt text to still images and GIFs. X supports descriptions up to 1,000
characters and recommends concise, objective descriptions that include
important visible text
([X: Accessibility features](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/accessibility-features),
[X: Writing image descriptions](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/write-image-descriptions)).

Non-Premium accounts can upload video up to 140 seconds, but X's organic
guidance recommends promotional videos of 15 seconds or less and captions or a
sound-off alternative for dialogue
([X: Longer videos](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/premium-longer-videos),
[X: Organic best practices](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices)).
Manual `.srt` caption upload is available on the web
([X: Upload captions](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/upload-caption-srt-file)).

### Cadence, scheduling, and conversation

X does not publish a universal numeric organic cadence in the current guidance
reviewed here. It says to share frequently and consistently, maintain a content
calendar and evergreen post bank, schedule for coverage, and test copy,
creative, and tone against the data
([X: Get your business started](https://business.x.com/en/basics/get-your-business-started-with-x),
[X: Organic best practices](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices)).

Standard posts can be scheduled from the web composer. Premium longer posts
currently cannot be saved as drafts or scheduled on web
([X: How to post](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/how-to-post),
[X: Types of posts](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/types-of-posts)).

X recommends engaging with other accounts to build relationships and
discoverability, replying to join conversations, using mentions to invite
someone relevant, monitoring indirect mentions, and responding with context
([X: Get your business started](https://business.x.com/en/basics/get-your-business-started-with-x),
[X: Organic best practices](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices)).
Do not paste promotional links into unrelated replies; X's authenticity policy
prohibits bulk, duplicative, and irrelevant promotional behavior
([X: Authenticity policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity)).

### Analytics

X's free Post Activity Dashboard is available after registering at
`analytics.x.com` or creating an Ads account. It reports impressions,
engagements, engagement rate, link clicks, reposts, likes, replies, and
post-level detail; it supports date ranges and CSV exports
([X: Post and Video Activity Dashboards](https://business.x.com/en/help/campaign-measurement-and-analytics/tweet-activity-dashboard)).

Public view counts are not unique reach: the author's own views and repeat
views can count, while embedded posts do not add views
([X: View counts](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/view-counts)).
Use link clicks and completed UTM-tagged site sessions as the primary traffic
pair, then use replies, reposts, profile visits, follows, and engagement rate to
explain why a post did or did not distribute.

X's dashboard, post list, and CSV can show different totals because they use
different date scopes; metrics may also fluctuate for up to 36 hours
([X: Analytics discrepancies](https://business.x.com/en/help/campaign-measurement-and-analytics/common-analytics-discrepancies)).

## Four-week measurement loop

This is a proposed experiment, not an official platform prescription:

1. Choose two LinkedIn posts per week and three X posts per article across
   7–10 days.
2. Give every social post a distinct UTM campaign/content value.
3. Change one variable at a time: hook, format, or CTA—not all three.
4. Record results after 24 hours, 72 hours, and 7 days.
5. Rank posts by qualified article visits first, then by conversation and
   out-of-network discovery.
6. Reuse the winning angle in a new format; do not repost identical copy.

Suggested scorecard:

| Outcome | LinkedIn | X | Site analytics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | Impressions, members reached, out-of-network share | Impressions, profile visits, follows | New users |
| Conversation | Comments, reposts, saves, sends | Replies, reposts, likes | — |
| Traffic | UTM campaign comparison | Link clicks | Sessions by UTM |
| Quality | Viewer role/industry where available | Engagement rate | Engaged time, scroll/completion, return visits |

After four weeks, keep the smallest cadence that reliably produces qualified
visits and genuine replies. Scale the winning format before increasing
frequency.
